# Harborlight Ledger

A free, private personal finance and debt payoff app for Windows. It helps you see what you owe, make a plan to get out of debt, and follow your progress.

**Your records stay on your computer, encrypted with your password. Nothing about you is sent anywhere.** There is no account with anyone else and no ads.

## Download

Go to **[Releases](../../releases/latest)** and download `Harborlight-Ledger-Setup-<version>.exe`, then run it.

Windows may show a SmartScreen warning the first time, because the installer is not signed with a paid certificate. Choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**.

## What it does

- **Dashboard** with your income, expenses, debt, a debt progress bar with milestones, and bills due soon.
- **Payoff Planner**: type an extra amount (or one total each month, or how many years you want to take) and see the date you could be debt free, what to pay each card this month, and a month-by-month table. A slider shows what $50 more or less would change.
- Credit cards with real interest, statement balances and the minimum your bank uses; mortgages, auto, student and personal loans, and medical bills.
- Bills with reminders, a budget, savings goals and net worth.
- Import transactions from a bank file (CSV).
- **English and Español** (Data & Backup, Language / Idioma). The Spanish is new: corrections are very welcome.
- Encrypted backups to a folder you choose, and a recovery code in case you forget your password.

## Privacy

Everything you enter is encrypted with your password and kept on your computer. The only time the app uses the internet is **Check for updates** (when you press it, or if you tick the box to check automatically): it asks GitHub which version is newest and sends nothing about you.

## Feedback

Found a mistake, a bad translation or an idea? Please open an **Issue** on this page. Nobody reads your records, so describe what you saw without private numbers.

## Not advice

Harborlight Ledger is a bookkeeping and planning tool, not financial, tax or legal advice. Loan and payoff figures are estimates: check them against your statements.
